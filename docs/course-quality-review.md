# Course quality review — 13 October 2026

Both courses are suitable as introductory applied practice after these revisions. They are not independently validated training, a comprehensive world-policy survey, or a qualification demonstrating professional competence. No learner study has yet established the advertised completion times or learning gains.

## What was reviewed and changed

Read all 24 lessons, exercises, rubrics, worked answers and 48 checks. Compared the methods and material factual claims with the relevant sections of the primary sources, including their dates, jurisdiction, authority and research limitations. Checked the fictional arithmetic separately. Reviewed NIST's full video transcript, playback duration and caption/transcript controls. Distinguish source-supported methods from AIPEX's proposed operating designs and invented case facts.

The original progression and bounded recommendations were sound. The weak points were obvious quiz distractors, insufficient evidence to analyse, short paragraph examples for structured exercises, broad reading directions and ambiguity between a draft/control promise and demonstrated performance. Changes:

- Every lesson now has a concrete fictional packet and a task applying its method to that packet. All 48 checks now test scenario reasoning, denominators, trade-offs or decision conditions.
- Worked answers now include explicit reasoning, tables where useful, and calculations. Capstone examples distinguish actual case observations from separate teaching scenarios; a pilot is no longer the assumed destination of discovery.
- Procurement responses, data-flow and permission controls, staff capacity, incidents, evidence status and competing manual alternatives are made operational.
- The policy course adds confusion-matrix metrics, descriptive group differences, a ten-day research allocation, incremental whole-service cost scenarios and a difference-in-differences example with causal assumptions.
- The services course distinguishes raw omissions (4/100), reviewer detection (3/4) and residual simulated omissions (1/100), demonstrates why zero misses in ten does not prove low risk, and costs the review rota (120 hours required against 80 allocated).
- Current legal/source scope is explicit: ICO ADM guidance remains labelled draft, DPIA guidance under review, Playbook legal wording is older, ATRS is scoped, and commercial playbooks do not establish today's statutory procurement route.
- One carefully selected official video is assigned in each course with different watch/apply tasks, a written alternative, and no embedded third-party player on AIPEX.
- Services workload estimate changes from 4–6 to 6–8 hours (385 scheduled minutes including the revised capstone). Policy remains 8–10 hours (545 scheduled minutes). Estimates include selected readings and substantive practice, not reading all publications cover to cover; actual learner timing remains unvalidated.

## Important source findings

- [Green Book 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-green-book--2/the-green-book-2026), updated 5 February 2026: objectives-led appraisal, realistic business as usual, social effects, distribution and balanced value-for-money judgment. A scoring table cannot replace this analysis.
- [Magenta Book 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/magenta-book-central-government-guidance-on-evaluation-html), updated May 2026, and [AI impact-evaluation guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/the-magenta-book/guidance-on-the-impact-evaluation-of-ai-interventions-html), updated 15 May 2026: evaluate the intervention, not merely technical benchmark performance. Verified latter text through GOV.UK HTML when web extraction timed out.
- [ICO ADM guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/individual-rights/automated-decision-making/): 31 March 2026 update reflects UK GDPR articles 22A–22D and is still explicitly **draft** at review. Its sections distinguish meaningful involvement before applying a decision from human intervention after an automated decision is challenged. [ICO DPIA guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/) explicitly carries a DUAA review notice.
- [AI Playbook](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ai-playbook-for-the-uk-government/artificial-intelligence-playbook-for-the-uk-government-html): operational controls remain relevant, but its Human oversight section contains older Article 22 language. Course readings identify this rather than presenting it as current law.
- [ATRS scope policy](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-mandatory-scope-and-exemptions-policy/algorithmic-transparency-recording-standard-atrs-mandatory-scope-and-exemptions-policy) and [Data and AI Ethics Framework](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/data-ethics-framework/data-and-ai-ethics-framework): organisational mandate, tool scope and exemptions are separate; local-government adoption is recommended rather than covered by a universal council mandate.
- [ICO 2024 recruitment audit](https://ico.org.uk/media2/migrated/4031620/ai-in-recruitment-outcomes-report.pdf): real examples motivate scrutiny of fairness, accuracy, transparency and supplier responsibility. Its selected consensual engagements excluded generative-AI tools and do not estimate market prevalence; its legal discussion predates DUAA.
- [Model Cards](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.03993), [Datasheets](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.09010) and [independent accountability research](https://www.adalovelaceinstitute.org/report/algorithmic-accountability-public-sector/): retained as original/historical research, not current legal authority or proof of control effectiveness. The latter report's North American/European focus limits transfer. The full report was inspected, not only its landing page.
- [NIST Generative AI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf), July 2024, §§2.2/2.9: adds a generative-system risk perspective beyond classifier documentation, including fabricated content and prompt injection. The course applies it to a fictional hostile enquiry and tests controls rather than assuming a defensive prompt works.
- [NIST video](https://www.nist.gov/video/introduction-nist-ai-risk-management-framework-ai-rmf-10-explainer-video): 26 January 2023, 6:31, full English transcript reviewed. Selected segments concern voluntary scope, actors and four functions. Future plans in that historical recording are not treated as current service availability. A candidate Turing lecture was not added because its transcript was unavailable for review; a platform-promotion video would add little to the exercises.

## Learning record compatibility

Lesson/course identifiers and written-response requirements remain stable. `quiz_revision` identifies saved option indexes; old answers are not preselected under different questions. Prior completion remains recorded. A stale open page receives a refresh/copy-work message before saving against changed questions. The additive database change preserves existing responses, scores and timestamps, and does not alter RLS or table privileges. Apply the migration and refresh the Data API schema before deploying.

## Remaining quality limits

The two UK-led fictional cases deliberately teach transferable practice; they do not cover every global institution, the EU AI Act, frontier-AI geopolitics or all sectors. Duration and assessment difficulty need observation with real learners. Written work is self-assessed and multiple-choice checks cannot establish professional competence. An independent policy practitioner and a small learner pilot would provide evidence this editorial review cannot. Recheck dated legal guidance when using the course for a real decision. Do not describe this review as external accreditation or a legal compliance review.


## Implementation validation

The six automated suites cover curriculum completeness, grading, redirect handling, question-edition compatibility, capstone example length and actual Markdown-table rendering across all lessons. Lint, TypeScript and production build pass. HTTP checks verify both first-lesson previews, the optional video and written alternative, absence of an embedded player, and all twelve teaching/packet sections plus the final case update in the services workbook. The database migration was applied to the dedicated AIPEX database and its Data API schema refreshed; the non-null revision default, existing RLS and three ownership policies were verified. Full live signup/email and two-user integration tests from launch were not rerun during this editorial review.
